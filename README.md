Project Overview & MotivationNearly one-third of global food production is lost or wasted annually, with post-harvest losses in perishable fruits and vegetables in India reaching $25\text{--}40\%$ due to inadequate real-time storage monitoring. Conventional quality assurance methods suffer from clear operational trade-offs:Visual & Olfactory Inspection: Subjective, non-quantitative, and only detects late-stage decay.Microbiological & Chemical Assays (TPC, TVBN, GC-MS, HPLC): Gold-standard accuracy, but destructive, expensive, slow, and confined to laboratory environments.Single-Sensor IoT Detectors: Prone to false alarms from ambient drift, cross-sensitivity, and poor generalization across diverse food biochemistry.Key ContributionsMulti-Modal Hardware Array: Integrates 4 complementary MOS gas sensors ($\text{MQ-3}$, $\text{MQ-4}$, $\text{MQ-5}$, $\text{MQ-135}$), an optical turbidity probe for liquid dairy, and a $\text{DHT11}$ ambient temperature/humidity sensor.Deterministic Sensor-Fusion Baseline ($SI$): Implements baseline calibration, Min-Max feature scaling, weighted linear sensor fusion, and inverse normalization to map heterogeneous sensor ranges onto an interpretable $[0, 1]$ spoilage scale.Cross-Category Longitudinal Dataset: Captures 175 day-wise observations tracking complete spoilage trajectories across 5 fruits, 5 vegetables, and 2 dairy products.Cloud-to-Web Telemetry Pipeline: Streams embedded sensor packets to ThingSpeak and surfaces day-wise progression, sensor trends, and JSON exports through a deployed Next.js dashboard.Research Positioning Note: While framed within an AI-IoT architecture, this v1 dissertation release establishes the deterministic signal-fusion baseline, hardware calibration workflow, and structured telemetry pipeline. Supervised machine learning classifiers (e.g., Random Forest, 1D-CNN, and shelf-life regression) are scoped for Phase 2 once multi-replicate datasets paired with laboratory microbiological ground truth (CFU / pH) are collected.2. Mathematical Model: Weighted Sensor Fusion & Spoilage IndexBecause individual MQ-series and turbidity sensors operate across incompatible numerical scales and baseline offsets, raw values are processed through a 4-stage analytical pipeline:Step 1: Baseline CalibrationClean-air / Day-1 fresh baseline readings ($S_{i,\text{base}}$) are recorded to isolate spoilage-driven signal deviations:$$\Delta S_i = S_i - S_{i,\text{base}}$$Step 2: Min-Max Normalization (Feature Scaling)Each sensor reading $S_i$ is mapped to a dimensionless fractional progression value $N_i \in [0, 1]$ using the baseline fresh reference ($S_{i,\text{base}}$) and confirmed spoiled peak ($S_{i,\text{spoil}}$):$$N_i = \text{clip}\left(\frac{S_i - S_{i,\text{base}}}{S_{i,\text{spoil}} - S_{i,\text{base}}},\ 0,\ 1\right)$$Step 3: Weighted Linear Sensor FusionNormalized outputs are fused into a single unified Spoilage Index ($SI$) bounded within $[0, 1]$:$$SI = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i N_i \quad \text{subject to} \quad \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i = 1$$Assigned Domain WeightsSensorTarget ModalityWeight (wi​) — Solid Foods (Gas Only)Weight (wi​) — Liquid Dairy (Gas + Turbidity)JustificationMQ-135$\text{NH}_3$ / Broad VOCs$0.35$$0.30$Strongest broad-spectrum microbial spoilage sensitivityMQ-3Ethanol / Alcohols$0.30$$0.25$Key early indicator of sugar & dairy fermentationMQ-5General VOCs / Hydrocarbons$0.20$$0.15$Cross-category volatile decomposition markerMQ-4Methane ($\text{CH}_4$)$0.15$$0.10$Late-stage anaerobic breakdown markerTurbidityOptical Scattering (NTU)N/A$0.20$Captures casein coagulation & physical curdling in liquidsTotal$1.00$$1.00$Step 4: Classification & Inverse NormalizationThe continuous $SI$ value maps into three operational freshness states:Spoilage Index (SI)ClassificationOperational Interpretation$0.0 \le SI < 0.3$FreshResponses within normal baseline variation; suitable for consumption$0.3 \le SI < 0.7$Early SpoilageMeasurable gas/matrix degradation initiated; prioritize consumption or discard soon$SI \ge 0.7$SpoiledAdvanced biochemical deterioration confirmed; Experimental Protocol & Environmental ConditionsMonitoring Period: November to February (ambient laboratory storage)Ambient Temperature: $18\text{--}28^\circ\text{C}$ (Mean $\approx 23^\circ\text{C}$)Ambient Relative Humidity: $45\text{--}75\%\text{ RH}$ (Mean $\approx 60\%\text{ RH}$)Chamber Protocol: Specimens were evaluated in a transparent, semi-enclosed chamber to accumulate headspace volatiles while limiting external room drafts. Solid items (fruits/vegetables) were centered beneath the headspace, while dairy liquids (milk/curd) were placed in sterile containers with the turbidity probe partially submerged.Sampling Cadence: Sensors underwent heater warm-up and clean-air baseline stabilization prior to logging; daily mean sensor responses were aggregated across the complete degradation window of each specimen (175 total day-item observations).5. Known Limitations & v2 Research RoadmapTransparent engineering documentation of current prototype boundaries and planned next-generation upgrades:Current v1 Prototype LimitationPlanned v2 Engineering / Research UpgradeNo Microbiological Ground Truth: Freshness boundaries were validated via gas accumulation and visual/organoleptic observation rather than lab assays.Pair sensor time-series with ISO 4833 Total Plate Count (CFU/g), pH/titratable acidity (for dairy), and headspace GC-MS to anchor $SI$ boundaries biologically.Offline Per-Specimen Normalization: Using each sample's own $S_{i,\text{base}}$ and $S_{i,\text{spoil}}$ is ideal for retrospective curve comparison, but live inference cannot know a sample's future peak.Freeze category-level calibration bounds ($S_{\text{cat,base}}, S_{\text{cat,spoil}}$) from multi-replicate training cohorts with strict $[0, 1]$ clamping during live inference.Deterministic Heuristic Weights: Weights ($0.35, 0.30, 0.20, 0.15$) were assigned via domain knowledge rather than learned from data.Train cross-validated Random Forest, Gradient Boosted Trees, or 1D-CNN models for both spoilage classification and Remaining Useful Shelf-Life (RUSL) regression.Uncompensated Environmental Drift: $\text{DHT11}$ logged ambient ranges ($18\text{--}28^\circ\text{C}$, $45\text{--}75\%\text{ RH}$), but temperature/humidity did not enter the $R_s/R_0$ equations.Upgrade to BME280/SHT31 and apply datasheet temperature-humidity compensation curves directly to sensor resistance calculations.Dashboard & Thesis Mode Alignment: The deployed Vercel demo uses conservative single-sensor triggers, whereas the thesis evaluates weighted $SI$ fusion.Add a UI toggle allowing users to switch between Weighted Fusion $SI$ (continuous $0.00\text{--}1.00$ score) and Strict Safety Alert (worst-sensor mode), plus live ThingSpeak streaming toggle.Chamber & Dairy Optical Physics: Semi-enclosed headspace and water-calibrated optical turbidity probes face saturation in opaque whole milk.Build a sealed acrylic manifold with a micro-circulation fan for gas arrays, and pair dairy monitoring with pH / electrical conductivity (EC) probes alongside headspace gas sensing.11.
